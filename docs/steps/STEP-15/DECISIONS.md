# STEP 15 - Decisions

D1 (2026-10-23). Where the code lives. `OWNERSHIP_MAP.md` gives STEP 15 no `lib/accounting/`, so the pure rules (input shapes, balance checks, CSV reading, tree, posting maths) live in `services/accounting/*-rules.ts` files that do not import `server-only`, and constants in `config/s15-accounting.ts` (the `sNN-` rule). Verification scripts are `scripts/verify/step15-*.ts`; strings will be `lib/i18n/strings/en/s15-accounting.ts` (15.4.b with the pages).

D2 (2026-10-23). Chart and journals (15.1). Migration `S15_001_chart_journals`. One chart per company; the platform has no shared chart. `accounts` has type, subtype (the pairs are checked in the database), a contra flag (accumulated depreciation, refunds), a generated `normal_side`, a parent (same company and type, at most 6 levels, no loop), `allow_posting` (false for group accounts) and system accounts (`is_system` with a `system_key`) that keep their code, type and key and can never be removed. An account with journal lines keeps its type and cannot be removed; it can be made inactive. Changes are written to the append-only `account_history`. `account_mappings` says which account a kind of transaction posts to (19 keys in `config/s15-accounting.ts`); the base chart fills them and the owner can re-map. A merge of two accounts moves the child accounts and the mappings to the target and makes the source inactive; posted lines are never touched, because history never changes (reports can group by the merge history in 15.4). The starting chart is the base chart plus an industry add-on (general, service, retail, medical, SaaS) and a country template that renames accounts to the words of US GAAP, IFRS or India (codes stay the same so reports stay comparable). The chart is imported from QuickBooks or Xero style CSV files and exported as CSV (a cell that starts with `=`, `+`, `-` or `@` is prefixed so a spreadsheet cannot run it).

D3 (2026-10-23). Entries (15.1). An entry is `draft`, `pending_approval` or `posted`. Debit equals credit, two lines at least and a total above zero are checked by a database trigger when an entry leaves draft, so no code path can post an unbalanced entry. The lines of an entry that is not a draft cannot change (insert, update and the soft delete of the guard are refused); a posted entry changes only to be marked as reversed. The number `JE-YYYY-NNNNNN` (year of the entry date) is given when the entry is posted (`kd_journal_number`, a counter per company and year). `kd_journal_create` makes the entry and its lines in one step; with a source key it makes one entry per key (a replay returns the first one), which is what the automated posting of 15.2 uses. A manual or imported entry with the same date and lines as an earlier live entry is refused with `KD001` unless the owner confirms (then it is linked in `duplicate_of`). Reversal (`kd_journal_reverse`) makes a new posted entry with debit and credit swapped, linked both ways, once only; a reversing entry cannot be reversed. A draft is replaced line by line (`kd_journal_replace`, with the version check) and can be removed; a posted entry cannot be removed. Approval: an owner posts at once; a staff member's post request becomes `pending_approval` with an approval request `journal.post` (level L1, 48 hours) and the owner decides; a rejection returns the entry to draft. Journal templates (reusable entries), CSV bulk import (creates drafts, one entry per reference) and attachments (storage contract, category `attachment`, PDF, PNG, JPEG or CSV up to 8 MB; a file of a posted entry cannot be removed) are in 15.1. Recurring entries (feature 2984) come with the periods job in 15.3 and the accountant review queue (3004) with automated posting in 15.2. The period check (feature 2991) is added by the trigger of 15.3.

D4 (2026-10-23). Automated posting (15.2). Migration `S15_002_posting`. The posting rules table is code (`services/accounting/posting-rules.ts`, `POSTING_RULES`), not a database table: it lists nine events and each one's lines by transaction key, and the company's `account_mappings` (15.1) turn the keys into accounts. A company that never opened accounting gets the starting chart the first time an event arrives; a company whose chart lacks a needed account gets it from the base chart (a free number if the usual one is taken). Two new keys: `wallet_balance` (1170, money settled into the company wallet and not yet paid out) and `chargeback_expense` (6410). One handler (`workers/s15/accounting-posting.event.ts`) reads the events; each event makes one entry, once per business source key (`invoice:<id>:sent`, `payment:<id>:captured`, `refund:<id>`, `payout:<id>:paid`, `dispute:<id>:lost`, `expense:<id>`, `payroll:<run>:approved|paid`), so a second delivery or a second event for the same thing changes nothing; the event id is kept in the posting log, which has one row per event and rule. The rules:
- Invoice: posted when it is sent (a draft is not revenue): debit receivable (total), credit revenue (total less tax), credit tax payable. A voided invoice reverses that entry (once; "never posted" and "already reversed" are logged as skipped). Withholding tax on an invoice is not split out (the receivable is the full total).
- Payment captured: the settlement of STEP 14 decides the books. Direct: debit bank, credit receivable. Through the wallet (including a suspended company, whose money the platform holds): debit wallet balance (net), debit payment processing fees (gross less net), credit receivable (gross). If the settlement is not recorded yet, the handler fails and the event is delivered again.
- Refund: debit refunds (contra revenue), credit bank (direct) or wallet balance. A fee returned by the platform is not booked separately.
- Payout paid: debit bank (net), debit processing fees (payout fee), credit wallet balance (amount). A failed or cancelled payout never reached the books; a payout reversed after payment has no event in the contract (request R1).
- Dispute lost: debit chargebacks, credit bank or wallet balance, amount from the dispute case.
- Expense and payroll: the payload is defined here and 15.5 and 15.8 must publish it (expense: `expenseId, date, net, tax, accountId?, paid, memo`; pay run: `runId, periodEnd, gross, employerCost, net` and `runId, net, paidOn`). Payroll liabilities hold net pay plus the tax and deductions withheld and the employer cost; paying the net pay clears part of it, the rest stays until it is remitted.
- Credit notes, debit notes, fixed assets, depreciation and FX gain or loss (features 2998 to 3002) need events the contract does not have or belong to 15.7; see R1.
A failed posting is logged with its reason and retried by the event runner; the owner sees failed and skipped rows, can retry one after fixing the chart, and reviews what the system posted (the accountant review queue, feature 3004: posted rows start unreviewed and are marked reviewed in bulk). Posting dates follow the event; a closed-period rule is added by 15.3.

D5 (2026-10-23). Periods (15.3). Migration `S15_003_periods`; `S15_001` gained the entry kind (`normal`, `adjusting`, `closing`) and the `p_kind` argument of `kd_journal_create` (the migration had not left this branch). A company sets its fiscal year start month (any of 12; it cannot change once a year exists) and the owner sets up a year, which makes 12 monthly periods (`kd_fiscal_year_create`, once per start date; years never overlap). A company with no periods is not restricted. The database refuses to post an entry (error `KD002`) when its date is in a closed period, in a period whose receivables or payables are locked and its source is of that kind (invoice, payment, refund, dispute: receivables; expense, pay run: payables), or on or before the lock date (a hard stop for backdating; the year-end closing entry is exempt). Drafts can still be made in a closed period. A month closes only when every entry dated in it is posted (database) and the generated checklist is done (no failed automatic posting, system entries reviewed); the person who closes it signs it off with a note. Reopening a month or year needs the owner, a fresh identity check (step-up within 10 minutes), a reason of at least 10 characters, and writes an audit entry and a notice; the reopen count is kept. A closed year refuses a month reopen: reopen the year, which reverses the closing entry and opens the months. This replaces an approval request for reopening: the platform has no way to resume an action after an approval by a person outside the company, and the step-up plus reason plus audit plus notice keeps the act traceable (recorded for the Integrator in R3). Year-end close: the checklist (all entries posted, no failed postings, year open) shows the result of the year; closing posts one entry dated the last day (key `yearend:<year>`, kind `closing`, left out of the reports of the year) that brings every revenue and expense account (contra accounts by the sign of their balance) to zero and moves the result to retained earnings, then closes every month and marks the year closed; each step can be repeated if the call stops half way. An event that falls in a closed period follows the company's policy: `next_open` (the default) posts it on the first day of the next open period and says so in the memo; `fail` leaves it failed in the posting log until the owner reopens the period. Adjusting entries are normal entries with the `adjusting` kind, listed apart in the reports. Recurring entries (monthly, quarterly, yearly; day 1 to 28) are made by a daily job as drafts or posted, one source key per run date, with catch-up of up to 12 runs and the last error kept on the entry and sent to the owner. Not built: the AR, AP and GL lock per module is AR and AP plus the whole month (GL); a separate GL-only lock is the month itself.

D6 (2026-10-23). Reports (15.4.a). Migration `S15_004_report_views` adds `accounts.is_cash` (cash and bank accounts; the starting chart marks bank, cash on hand and the wallet balance) and two SQL functions: `kd_ledger_balances` (debit and credit per account for posted entries in a date range, closing entries optional) and `kd_ledger_monthly` (per month, closing entries left out). All reports are pure functions in `services/accounting/report-rules.ts` over the account list and those sums; `reports.ts` reads the ledger and calls them. Fifteen reports are served by `GET /api/accounting/reports/<key>` (catalogue at `/api/accounting/reports`): trial balance, general ledger, account register, profit and loss (with a comparison period), profit and loss by month, balance sheet (with a comparison date), cash flow, receivables aging, payables aging, cash balances, tax summary (tax collected against tax paid on purchases, which also serves VAT in and out, feature 1166), financial ratios, expense summary, expenses by account and expense trend by month. Drill-down from a report line to the journal is the register of that account (every line carries the entry id). Rules: only posted entries count; the profit and loss of a period leaves closing entries out, balances include them, so equity is right before and after the year is closed (the balance sheet adds the result of revenue and expense accounts that no closing entry has taken yet as "Current year earnings", and `balanced` checks assets equal liabilities plus equity). A contra account (accumulated depreciation, refunds) reduces its section and shows as a negative line. The cash flow statement uses the indirect method: net income, depreciation added back, changes in other current assets and liabilities (operating), fixed and other assets (investing), loans and equity (financing); a direct booking to retained earnings shows as "Other equity movements"; the statement reports its own difference from the change in cash (zero when it ties out). Aging buckets are current, 1-30, 31-60, 61-90, 91-120 and over 120 days past the due date; receivables come from the open invoices of STEP 09 and payables from the open supplier bills of STEP 08, which hold today's amounts, so an aging as of a past date is an estimate (stated in the answer). A report covers at most about two years; the general ledger is limited to 5000 lines and says when it was cut. Not in this sub-step: profit and loss by department, cost centre or project (the ledger lines have no such dimension; a request if wanted, R4), the cash flow projection (30, 60, 90 days) and fixed asset, depreciation, budget and variance reports (15.7), scheduled e-mail of a report and chart images (15.4.b with the pages), PDF, CSV and Excel export (15.4.b).

D7 (2026-10-23). Accounting pages and exports (15.4.b). Pages under `/accounting`: overview (cash, revenue and result of the year, entries to review, failed postings, drafts), chart of accounts (tree, the standard chart for a company with none, add, deactivate, CSV import and export), journal entries (filters, new entry, entry page with edit of a draft, approve or reject, reverse, files), periods (years, months, locks, checklists, year close and reopen, settings), recurring entries, automatic postings (review queue and the posting rules table) and the reports (a catalogue and a viewer with period or date options, a comparison with the same period last year for profit and loss). The journal editor shows debit, credit and the difference while the person types and posts only when they are equal; a duplicate is offered as "save anyway". Every report can be downloaded as CSV, Excel or PDF: `report-tables.ts` turns each report into the flat table (`ReportResult`) that the platform's report file builder (STEP 09) draws, so the export carries an export id and the name of the person, and `accounting.report_exported` is written to the audit log (the chart CSV writes `accounting.chart_exported`). A file of a journal entry opens through a plain route that sends the person to a short-lived signed link of their own company's file. A staff member sees the same pages; buttons that need the owner's permission (chart changes, posting approval, periods) are shown only to those who have it, and the server checks again. Scheduled e-mail of a report (feature 3013) is not built here: the platform's report schedule (STEP 09) cannot take an accounting report without a change in STEP 09 (request R5). Charts (features 3012, 3020) are not drawn; the monthly profit and loss table gives the same figures. Pages use the small shared form and button components of the wallet pages (STEP 14) instead of copies.

D8 (2026-10-23). Expenses (15.5). Migration `S15_005_expenses`. Categories nest four levels, each can name an expense account of the chart (a child uses the nearest account above it; none means the default expense account) and says whether the spending is tax deductible; the usual categories are added on first use. An expense has a net amount, tax, a total (the database checks total = net + tax), a vendor, an optional receipt, and two separate states: approval (`pending`, `approved`, `rejected`) and payment (`unpaid`, `paid`). Policies (per category and its children, per role) set the most for one expense, per person per day and per month, a receipt rule above an amount, and an amount below which an expense is approved by itself; a breach is flagged (the expense waits for the owner, who must give a reason of at least 10 characters to approve it) or blocked (refused; only an owner with such a reason may go past it). A new expense: an owner's is approved at once unless a policy was breached; a staff member's is approved at once only when a policy says so and nothing was breached, otherwise it waits for the owner through the approval service (`expense.approve`, level L1, 72 hours). Staff see and change only their own expenses. Duplicates: the same date and total with the same receipt checksum (SHA-256 of the file), or without a receipt the same vendor; the person is asked to confirm and the new expense is linked to the first. An approved expense cannot change its date, amounts or category and cannot be deleted (the database refuses); a pending or rejected one can be changed (it goes back to pending) or deleted. When an expense is approved the event `expense.created` is published with the payload fixed in D4 (accounting books it: debit the category account and tax receivable, credit the bank if it was paid, else accounts payable); an approved expense whose event was missed is published again by the daily job. Paying an owed expense (`markPaid`) books debit accounts payable, credit bank, once per expense, and then marks it paid; this is also the reimbursement of an employee who paid (feature 2940 to 2943, "reimbursable" is a flag and the payment is a bank payment; adding the payment to payroll (2942) is built with the pay runs in 15.8, which will settle such expenses and set the way to payroll). Receipts go through the storage contract (category `receipt`, retention 10 years in the storage settings); `applyOcrResult(companyId, receiptId, fields)` is the connection point for the OCR service of another STEP and the form offers the read values as a suggestion. Recurring expenses make an approved expense on each due date (one per item and date, database enforced, with the expenses made as the history), a failed run is kept on the item and sent to the owner. Reports (approved expenses only, as flat tables exported as CSV, Excel and PDF): summary by month with the amount waiting, by category with each parent including its children, by employee, tax deductibility and policy breaches; the trend by month is the accounting report from 15.4. Not built: approval limits per approver and a manager level (the platform has owner and staff only; one level), expense by department (R4), expense against budget (15.7), and a per-trip limit (no trip concept).

D9 (2026-10-23). Bank reconciliation (15.6). Migration `S15_006_reconciliation`. A bank account is tied to a cash account of the chart (the database refuses any other) and keeps only the last 4 digits of the number, an opening balance and its date. Statements come in as CSV, OFX or QFX files, or as a batch from the bank feed service of STEP 16 (`importFeedBatch(companyId, bankAccountId, batchId, lines)`; a repeated batch id is refused). CSV columns are found by name (date, description, reference, one amount column or debit and credit columns, balance); the owner picks the date format (year-month-day, month/day/year or day/month/year); amounts like `1,234.56`, `(12.00)` or `12.00-` are read; a bad line is listed with its line number and the good lines are kept; OFX needs USD. The same file is refused twice (file hash); a line already imported, even from an overlapping file, is skipped (a key from the account, date, amount, text and reference with the place among identical lines, or the bank's own transaction id); only lines are stored, not the file. A bank line is `unmatched`, `matched` (its matches add up to its amount; one bank line can match several journal lines and a journal line matches once, checked by the database, which also checks the journal line is posted and on the ledger account of the bank account) or `ignored`. Suggestions score a pair of the same amount by date distance (up to 45 days) and shared words or the reference in the entry; "match all clear suggestions" matches a line only when its best candidate scores 60 or more and beats the next by 10, and never uses a journal line twice. A line with no entry in the books is booked to an account (fee, interest): the entry is made as a system entry (key `bank:<line>:booked`), posted and matched at once; bank rules do the same at import when the text contains the rule's words (per account and direction). A reconciliation is for a statement date and balance; it opens at the ending balance of the last completed one (or the account's opening balance); only one is open per account; it can be finished when every line up to the date is matched or ignored and the lines add up to the change on the statement (difference zero), and finishing ties the lines to it so they and their matches can no longer change. The books side is shown beside it: ledger balance at the date (opening balance plus the postings since the opening date), the entries the bank has not shown yet, and the books-against-statement discrepancy; it is information, the gate is the lines. The report (summary, the lines cleared and the entries not shown) and the history of all reconciliations are available as CSV, Excel and PDF. Locking the accounting period at completion (feature 3051) is not done: the lock belongs to the period close of 15.3, which now checks nothing about reconciliation; recorded as a possible checklist item for the month close. Bank feeds themselves (feature 3042) are STEP 16.

D10 (2026-10-23). Fixed assets (15.7.a). Migration `S15_007_assets`. Asset categories (equipment, furniture, vehicles, software, buildings; the usual ones are added on first use) give the usual method and life and may name the asset, accumulated depreciation (a contra asset) and depreciation expense accounts; none means the company's default accounts (mapping keys `fixed_assets`, `accumulated_depreciation`, `depreciation_expense`). A new key `disposal_gain_loss` (account 7200, a gain is a credit, a loss a debit) was added to the base chart and the mappings. An asset has cost, salvage value, life in months, one of four methods (straight line; declining balance with a factor from 1 to 4, on the net book value, so it falls each month; sum of the digits; units of production), an in-service date (depreciation starts in that month, a full month), place, serial number, vendor, warranty and photos or documents (storage contract, category `attachment`). The purchase is booked only when the owner says how it was paid (bank or payables); an asset already on the books is added with nothing posted (also the CSV import). The last month always takes what is left down to the salvage value, so the total is exact; no method goes below the salvage value. The database refuses a change of cost, salvage, life, method, start date, factor, units or category after depreciation exists, and any change of a sold or disposed asset. Depreciation: the daily job (`s15-assets`) and the owner's button book every month up to the end of last month, oldest first, as one entry per company and month (source key `asset-depreciation:<company>:<month>`, grouped by the accounts of the categories) plus one row per asset and month (append only, unique per asset and month), so running a month twice books it once. A closed month stops the run with the reason kept in the answer; the next run continues. Units of production books a month only when the owner has recorded the units used. Disposal (owner, fresh identity check, idempotency key): depreciation is brought up to the month before the date, then one entry (key `asset-disposal:<asset>`) debits the bank (proceeds) and accumulated depreciation, credits the asset account at cost, and books the difference between the proceeds and the net book value as a gain or a loss; the asset becomes `sold` (proceeds above zero) or `disposed`. Reports (flat tables, exported as CSV, Excel and PDF with an audit entry): the register as of a date, depreciation by category and month, assets by category, disposals with gain or loss, warranties. Not built: partial-month depreciation conventions (mid-month, half-year), revaluation (feature 3070), asset transfer between companies, a tax depreciation book next to the accounting book, and the scheduled warranty reminder e-mail.

D11 (2026-10-23). Budgets, forecast, business intelligence and multi-entity (15.7.b). Migration `S15_008_budgets_entities`. Budgets: a twelve month plan of revenue and expense accounts, one amount per account and month. The owner or staff type 12 monthly, 4 quarterly or 1 yearly amount (a quarterly amount is split over three months and a yearly one over twelve, the remainder in the last month, so the total is exact). The status moves one way and the database enforces it: draft, pending approval, approved, locked; an approved budget is replaced (`superseded`) when its approved revision takes its place; a locked budget never changes and cannot be revised; only a draft budget accepts line changes; one approved or locked budget per company and year. A staff member submits and the owner decides through the approval service (`budget.approve`, level L1, 72 hours); a rejection returns the draft with the reason. A copy from another budget moves the amounts by whole months and changes them by a percent. The variance report compares the budget with the posted ledger for the months up to the one asked for (variance is positive when better than the budget), flags expense accounts at 80 and 100 percent used (an expense with no budget is flagged as over), and projects the year from the average of the last three months. The daily job (`s15-budgets`) records one alert row per budget, account, month and level (unique, append only) and sends the notice once. Not built: budgets per department, cost centre or project (the ledger lines carry no such dimension, request R4). Forecast: the cash projection for 30, 60 and 90 days starts from the cash accounts of the ledger and adds open invoices (on their due date, or late ones the owner's number of days after today), subtracts open supplier bills, approved expenses not yet paid and the recurring expenses due, by week; it shows the lowest point, the monthly burn of the last three full months and the runway in months. The owner sets a warning level; the job warns at most once a week. Revenue and expense forecasts are a least squares line through the last six months (average under three months, never below zero); a revenue forecast from a sales pipeline is not built (there is no pipeline in the platform) and payroll is not part of the projection until 15.8 books it. Business intelligence (the last twelve months): margins, top clients by revenue with what is paid and owed (expenses are not tied to clients, so no profit per client), change against last month, quarter and year, the seasonal pattern, break-even (cost of sales as variable cost and all other expenses as fixed), working capital, current and quick ratio, debt to equity, return on equity and on assets, and a health score from 0 to 100 (liquidity 25, profit 25, debt 15, cash 20, collections 15 percent) with plain-sentence insights and up to five coach tips from fixed rules. Not built: profit margin per product or service and project ROI (no such dimension in the ledger, R4). Every table can be downloaded as CSV, Excel and PDF with an audit entry. Multi-entity: the platform's tenant is a company, so one owner's legal entities are several companies. A group is led by one company (one group per company); a company joins only when the person is an active owner of it (checked in the service at joining and again at every read of the consolidated statements, with a step-up on adding). Intercompany pairs name one account in each of two companies of the group (the database checks the accounts belong to their companies and mirror each other); consolidation lines up the accounts of all members by type and code, eliminates the smaller of the two sides of each pair from both and shows the difference as unreconciled, consolidates a part-owned member in full and shows the share of the other owners in its result. The consolidated profit and loss and balance sheet are shown and downloaded; the balance sheet checks assets against liabilities, equity and the result not yet closed. All money is USD, so there is no currency translation (feature 3095 needs no work). Not built: multiple books under different accounting standards, GAAP and IFRS compliance checks, the cash or accrual toggle, accrued and deferred revenue and prepaid expense tracking (features 3081 to 3089; the chart wording of US GAAP, IFRS and India is in 15.1), and the accounting standard selection beyond that.

D12 (2026-10-23). Tax filing support and the audit pack (15.7.c). Migration `S15_009_tax_audit` (named after the order of building; the payroll migrations of 15.8 follow it). Tax reports (CSV, Excel and PDF, each export audited) read the tax breakdown that every invoice keeps (code, rate, taxable amount, tax) for invoices that were sent (not draft, voided or rejected), by invoice date; the jurisdiction of a tax code comes from the country and region of its tax rate (`US-CA`, `GB`, or `Unspecified` when the rate names no country, which the report says). Reports: sales tax by jurisdiction and tax code (a code that changed rate during the period shows once per rate); the VAT and GST return (output tax of VAT and GST codes against input tax from supplier bills by line rate and approved expenses, net payable, negative means a refund; input tax is not split by jurisdiction); withholding by payee (the client) from the withholding on invoices; the quarterly tax provision (the owner's rate on each quarter's profit before tax from the ledger, a loss gives nothing and is not carried forward, an estimate and not a return); tax paid against tax due by jurisdiction (tax charged, before credit for input tax, against the payments recorded); and the filing data of one jurisdiction (the sales tax lines in a plain format, not a form of any authority; jurisdiction-specific formats, feature 3119, are not built). Tax payments and a deadline calendar are records the owner keeps; recording a payment does not post to the books (the owner makes the journal entry, which keeps the books under their control). The daily job (`s15-tax`) reminds before a deadline (owner's days, default 7) and on the day, once each, until it is marked done; moving a deadline or reopening it restarts the reminders (database). Not built: payroll tax per quarter, US 1099 and W-2 exports and the payroll hook (features 3112 to 3114 and 3133) because they need the payroll data of 15.8; they are added there. Audit pack: the owner, after a fresh identity check, downloads one gzip file of JSON for a period of at most about a year: the chart, the chart changes, journal entries with who made, posted and reversed them, their lines, the attachment names, the fiscal periods with close and reopen facts, the audit trail rows of the company (each with its previous hash and hash) and the manifest with the SHA-256 of every section and the result of the audit chain check over the whole chain (with its head). The manifest checksum, the file checksum and the chain result are kept in an append-only record of each pack, and making a pack is audited. The pack lists the files attached to entries by name and id but does not contain them. An auditor recomputes the section checksums from the file (`checkPack`). The audit trail itself, its seven year retention and its access control belong to STEP 02 and the platform (features 3121, 3129, 3130); the journal, chart and period audits are the audit rows and histories the earlier sub-steps write. Not built: accountant review mode per period (3126), the periodic review schedule (3127), petty cash (3131) and check printing (3132).

D13 (2026-10-23). Payroll people and calculation (15.8.a). Migration `S15_010_payroll_people` (numbered after `S15_009`, which 15.7.c took). Pure rules are in `services/payroll/*-rules.ts` (D1; `lib/payroll/` is not in the ownership map). Employees: plain columns for the profile (name, email, phone, address, date of birth, department, title, join and end date, contract type, pay basis, grade, structure, status, emergency contact, custom fields of up to 20 keys, avatar file id) and three encrypted payloads (`pay_enc`: salary or hourly rate and commission percent; `bank_enc`: holder, bank, account number, routing code; `tax_enc`: tax id, filing status, allowances) sealed with the platform's encryption service (purpose `pii`, scoped to the company). The number `EMP-0001` is given by the database per company (advisory lock, never changes); an email is unique among the employees who are not terminated. A terminated employee needs a last day; only a terminated employee can be archived, and the pay history stays. Field-level access: the profile needs `payroll.employee.read`, the pay, bank and tax groups need `payroll.pay.read` (shown masked: the account number and tax id show only the last four characters), changing them needs `payroll.pay.manage` and a fresh identity check, the pay run code reads the full values through `payDetails` which no route exposes. Every view of an employee is written to the audit log with the groups that were shown; changes list the fields (encrypted ones as "encrypted"); the directory export, document opens and pay previews are audited. All five permissions default to the owner; the owner can grant them to staff. The CSV import takes the profile only (no pay, bank or tax identity); the export leaves them out. Documents go through the storage contract and open by a signed link. Salary structure: grades (a range), components (earning or deduction; fixed per month, percent of basic, or for a deduction percent of gross; taxable or not; a deduction can be taken before tax), structures with a value per component that replaces the default, and payroll settings (pay frequency weekly, every two weeks, twice a month or monthly; overtime multiplier 1 to 5 and an optional limit of hours per pay period; standard hours per month; rounding none, cent or dollar). Payroll tax: the platform ships no rates. The owner enters rules: flat (a rate on taxable or gross pay, with an optional yearly wage cap and an extra rate above a yearly threshold, which is how social security and medicare are built) or bracket (annual brackets applied to the annualised taxable pay after a yearly exemption); the kind is income tax, employee contribution or employer contribution (a cost of the company that does not reduce net pay). Caps and thresholds work on the year to date. Calculation (pure): basic pay (monthly salary scaled to the pay frequency, or hourly rate times hours), commission (sales times percent), overtime (hourly rate, or monthly salary over the standard hours, times the multiplier, limited by the cap with a warning), component earnings and bonus and incentive, gross; taxable pay is gross less non-taxable earnings and pre-tax deductions; deductions; taxes; net. Someone who joins or leaves in the period is paid for the days employed (days paid over days in the period, multiplied first so no cent is lost; found by the check script). Every line is rounded by the company's rule before it is added, so the lines of a payslip add up exactly to its totals. Net pay below zero is kept and flagged; the pay run (15.8.b) must refuse it. The pay preview calculates one employee for one period without saving; the year to date is taken as zero there and the pay runs will pass the real year to date. Pay is in USD only (feature 2830). Not built: conditional components (feature 2820 "conditional"), the US federal and state specifics as built-in tables (the owner enters brackets), W-4 style declarations by the employee (the self-service portal is 15.9), tax filing exports for payroll (15.8.b), annual bracket update reminders (2842), and the avatar upload (the column exists; the upload is not built).
