D2 (2026-10-18). The other baseline steps are run by hand and pass: `npm run lint`, `npm run typecheck`, `scripts/check-all.sh`.

D3 (2026-10-18). What the adapters cannot do. Found while planning: every payment adapter reports `supportsRecurring: false` and `supportsPayout: false`, and the adapter contract has no operation for a stored payment method, a charge against it, or a payout. A cross-STEP interface is never invented, and processor code is never written without the processor's official documentation (rule 8). So: (1) 14.3 builds the payout framework with a partner interface of its own (`lib/payment/payout.ts`) and the manual partner (the platform team pays by bank transfer outside the platform and marks the payout paid), which needs no vendor API; a live partner adapter is a request (R3) that needs the partner's documentation. (2) 14.8 makes and sends the invoice of every cycle and lets the client pay the link; charging a stored method automatically waits for adapter support (R3). (3) 14.9 builds eligibility, offer data and installment tracking; a BNPL partner call waits for documentation.

D4 (2026-10-18). Plan keys. `config/plans.ts` (STEP 01) fixes `free`, `pro`, `business`, `enterprise`; `companies.plan_key` accepts any snake-case token, and STEP 10 routing rules list those four keys. The feature list names the plans Free, Starter, Pro and Enterprise; the existing four keys are kept as the stable slugs (renaming would break routing rules and stored plan keys). The database `plans` table is seeded from the config; the platform team edits prices, limits, features, trial days, visibility and order, can archive a plan, and can add extra plans with their own slugs. A plan that is not one of the four built-in keys cannot appear in a routing rule until STEP 10 relaxes its list (R5).

D5 (2026-10-18). Modes. Processor credentials are platform-level (entered on the super admin page of STEP 10; owners never enter keys), so every online payment reaches the platform's own processor account. Whether the platform then holds the money for the company depends on the mode of that company: `mor` (the platform is merchant of record: the payment is settled into the company wallet net of fees, after the hold period; needs the platform toggle, the company toggle and a verified KYC), `direct` (the money does not pass through a wallet; the platform only records its fee for the monthly fee invoice; this is the mode while the platform is not legally ready to hold funds, controlled by the setting `mor_platform_enabled`, default off) and `suspended` (risk or expired KYC: no new settlement; existing holds stay). The mode is decided in one place (`services/mor/mode.ts`). A gate STEP 10 can ask before taking a payment is provided as a service function; the call from STEP 10 is a request (R2).

D6 (2026-10-18). Names. Rule 18 applies to code, strings and these documents: payment partners are "Global Payout Partners" or "Local Payment Methods via Licensed Partners"; method codes are internal (`mobile_wallet_a`, `mobile_wallet_b`, `bank_transfer_local`, `instant_bank_qr` and so on) with generic labels. The feature list text itself, which lives in `docs/source/`, is the only place that carries the brand words.

D8 (2026-10-19). Ledger design (14.1). Migration `S14_001_wallet_ledger`. A posting (`wallet_postings`, with the idempotency key and a hash of the whole request) has two to twelve legs (`ledger_entries`) that add up to zero; a deferred constraint trigger checks this when the transaction ends, so even a direct insert cannot leave an unbalanced posting. Legs on a company account (`available`, `held`) carry a wallet; legs on the platform accounts (`processor_receivable`, `platform_fee_revenue`, `processor_fee_expense`, `payout_in_transit`, `clearing`) do not. Positive on a company account means the platform owes the company; the platform legs carry the opposite sign. The two balances sit on the wallet row for speed, change only inside `kd_wallet_post` (a trigger refuses any other write), and `kd_wallet_verify()` proves they equal the ledger sums; the assertion and the parallel script both run it. Safety against overdraw is by row locks taken in id order inside the function (not by the serializable isolation level, which PostgREST cannot set per call): two debits of 40 against 100 at the same time give one success and one refusal, never 120 out of 100. The same key is serialized by an advisory lock, so twelve identical requests make one posting and eleven replays (the parallel script found the race that a plain existence check would have had). A debit may take the available balance below zero only when the leg says `floor: false` (a chargeback); a later credit pays the negative balance back automatically. Holds (`wallet_holds`) are posted together with the move from available to held and are released by `kd_wallet_release_hold`, which is safe to call twice.

D9 (2026-10-19). Fees (14.1). `lib/payment/fee-calculator.ts` has four models (flat, percent, percent plus fixed, tiered where the tier of the payment prices the whole payment) with a minimum and a maximum, a schedule with a default model, models per processor and a tax rate on the platform fee, and a breakdown (gross, platform fee, tax, processor fee, what is taken, net). The processor's own fee is a cost of the platform: it is shown but not taken from the company. A fee never exceeds the payment. The platform default is stored in `fee_schedules` (company null) and a company can have its own schedule; both are set only by the super admin (checked in `services/wallet/fees.ts`, audited by the table trigger). Until the platform team sets one, the built-in default is two percent with no fixed part and no tax; it is a starting value, not a price list. Fee history report, the monthly fee invoice and the screens come with 14.2, 14.3.b and 14.6.

D10 (2026-10-19). Wallet alerts and export (14.1). After a posting the owner is told once when the available balance goes below zero and once when it falls below the threshold they set (stored on the wallet). The ledger CSV neutralises cells a spreadsheet would run as formulas. The monthly statement PDF is drawn with the owner page in 14.3.b.

D11 (2026-10-19). Settlement design (14.2). Migration `S14_002_settlement`. A captured payment is settled by `services/mor/settle.ts` into two postings with keys made from the payment (`settle:<payment>` and `settle:<payment>:reserve`): the company receives the payment minus the platform fee and its tax (`held`, or `available` when the hold is zero days), the platform keeps the fees (`platform_fee_revenue`) and the processor owes the whole payment (`processor_receivable`). A reserve (0 to 50 percent of what the company receives, default 0) is a second hold with a later release time (the reserve days). The settlement item (`settlement_items`, one per payment) keeps gross, fee, tax, net, reserve, refunded, charged back, the hold and the reason text the owner sees. Hold length: the standard hold (default 7 days); a company younger than the new-merchant age (default 30 days) gets the longer new-merchant hold (default 14 days); a high automatic risk score adds the risk extension (default 7 days). A refund takes back what the company received for that share of the payment (the platform returns its fee in the same share, once in total) and may take the available balance below zero (later payments pay it back first); a lost dispute takes the disputed amount (not the fee) the same way. Replays are safe because every posting key is made from the payment, refund or dispute id.

D12 (2026-10-19). Mode and risk (14.2). `services/mor/mode.ts` is the one place that decides `mor`, `direct` or `suspended` (rules in `lib/payment/mor-engine.ts`): suspended flag wins; the platform switch (default off) and the company switch (default on) must be on; a verified KYC gives `mor`; an ended verification gives `suspended` (point 1522); missing, pending or rejected KYC gives `direct` with a clear message. A payment captured in `direct` mode is recorded with its fee (for the fee invoice of 14.6); in `suspended` mode it is recorded as `pending` and settled by the hourly job when the mode allows. The contract `kyc` cannot tell an ended verification from one never sent, so `mode.ts` reads `kyc_submissions.expires_at` (read only). Risk is rule based (large payment, payments in 24 hours, daily amount, disputes in 90 days, refund share, new merchant); a score of 70 or more is high: the hold is extended, and when `auto_suspend` is on (default) settlement of new payments is paused and the owner is told. Money already held is never touched by a suspension. Only the super admin changes the settings, suspends or reinstates, releases a hold early or extends one; each action is audited. The MoR agreement e-sign and legal pages (FEATURE_LIST 1513 to 1515) are not in this sub-step; they need the contract and legal page work of the owner pages and are listed in the closure handoff.

D13 (2026-10-19). Reading other STEPs' tables (14.2). `payments` (STEP 10), `disputes` (STEP 13), `companies` and `kyc_submissions` are read only, as STEP 13 read `payments`. No row of those tables is written. The gate for the payment flow is `settlementGate(companyId)` in `services/mor/mode.ts` (request R2).

D14 (2026-10-19). Payouts (14.3.a). Migration `S14_003_payouts`. A payout request does these checks in order: permission, a fresh sign-in confirmation (10 minutes), verified KYC, minimum and fee, active method, active wallet, the period limit (the request row is written first and counted, so two requests at once cannot both pass it) and the funds. The funds leave the available balance at the request (`available` to `payout_in_transit`, the fee to `platform_fee_revenue`), so two requests cannot spend the same money (the ledger locks the wallet). Approval: a payout at or above the large threshold (default 5,000 USD) always waits for the CEO (`payout.large`, level L2, a second person); a staff request below it waits for the owner (`payout.request`, L1); an owner request below it needs none. A rejected, cancelled or failed payout credits the money back once (key `payout:<id>:back`); a paid payout moves the net from `payout_in_transit` to `clearing`; a payout whose money came back after being sent is `reversed` by the super admin. Failed attempts are tried again after 1, 6 and 24 hours (four attempts in all), then the payout fails and the money returns; the platform team can end the retries at once. The FX rate is taken from the `fx` contract at the request and kept on the payout with the local amount (conversion margin from the payout settings). Starting values live in `config/s14-payouts.ts` (minimum 10 USD, limit 10,000 USD per day, fee 0, large threshold 5,000 USD) until the platform team saves its own. The only partner is the manual one (the platform team pays by bank transfer and confirms in the queue with the bank reference); a live partner needs its official documentation and the adapter contract of R3, and `partnerFor` already falls back to the manual partner when a partner is not healthy. A scheduled payout (daily at 06:00 UTC, weekly, or the 1st to 28th of the month) takes the available balance above the amount to keep, stays below the large threshold, and has a key made from the schedule and its run time. Account details are encrypted with purpose `pii` and the company as scope; pages see only "ending 1234". Partner reconciliation, quota monitor and settlement report of a partner (FEATURE_LIST 1372 to 1374) have no live partner to reconcile with and are not built; the queue screens and the payout emails come with 14.3.b.
