# STEP 14 - Decisions

D1 (2026-10-18). Branch base. `origin/integration` holds only STEP 01; STEPS 02 to 13 live on the session branch `claude/clever-goldberg-8ncdae`. STEP 14 consumes contracts implemented by STEPS 02 to 10 and the services of 09, 10 and 13, so it continues on this branch (as STEPS 11 to 13 did). The branch already contains the latest `origin/integration`.

D2 (2026-10-18). Baseline. `scripts/integration-check.sh --quick` stops at its first step: the generated barrels `config/nav-map.ts` and `config/permissions.ts` are out of date (the Integrator regenerates them; this STEP writes fragment files only, and `npm run gen` is run only to refresh the barrels that earlier STEPs also committed). The other baseline steps are run by hand and pass: `npm run lint`, `npm run typecheck`, `scripts/check-all.sh`.

D3 (2026-10-18). What the adapters cannot do. Found while planning: every payment adapter reports `supportsRecurring: false` and `supportsPayout: false`, and the adapter contract has no operation for a stored payment method, a charge against it, or a payout. A cross-STEP interface is never invented, and processor code is never written without the processor's official documentation (rule 8). So: (1) 14.3 builds the payout framework with a partner interface of its own (`lib/payment/payout.ts`) and the manual partner (the platform team pays by bank transfer outside the platform and marks the payout paid), which needs no vendor API; a live partner adapter is a request (R3) that needs the partner's documentation. (2) 14.8 makes and sends the invoice of every cycle and lets the client pay the link; charging a stored method automatically waits for adapter support (R3). (3) 14.9 builds eligibility, offer data and installment tracking; a BNPL partner call waits for documentation.

D4 (2026-10-18). Plan keys. `config/plans.ts` (STEP 01) fixes `free`, `pro`, `business`, `enterprise`; `companies.plan_key` accepts any snake-case token, and STEP 10 routing rules list those four keys. The feature list names the plans Free, Starter, Pro and Enterprise; the existing four keys are kept as the stable slugs (renaming would break routing rules and stored plan keys). The database `plans` table is seeded from the config; the platform team edits prices, limits, features, trial days, visibility and order, can archive a plan, and can add extra plans with their own slugs. A plan that is not one of the four built-in keys cannot appear in a routing rule until STEP 10 relaxes its list (R5).

D5 (2026-10-18). Modes. Processor credentials are platform-level (entered on the super admin page of STEP 10; owners never enter keys), so every online payment reaches the platform's own processor account. Whether the platform then holds the money for the company depends on the mode of that company: `mor` (the platform is merchant of record: the payment is settled into the company wallet net of fees, after the hold period; needs the platform toggle, the company toggle and a verified KYC), `direct` (the money does not pass through a wallet; the platform only records its fee for the monthly fee invoice; this is the mode while the platform is not legally ready to hold funds, controlled by the setting `mor_platform_enabled`, default off) and `suspended` (risk or expired KYC: no new settlement; existing holds stay). The mode is decided in one place (`services/mor/mode.ts`). A gate STEP 10 can ask before taking a payment is provided as a service function; the call from STEP 10 is a request (R2).

D6 (2026-10-18). Names. Rule 18 applies to code, strings and these documents: payment partners are "Global Payout Partners" or "Local Payment Methods via Licensed Partners"; method codes are internal (`mobile_wallet_a`, `mobile_wallet_b`, `bank_transfer_local`, `instant_bank_qr` and so on) with generic labels. The feature list text itself, which lives in `docs/source/`, is the only place that carries the brand words.

OWNERSHIP_MAP row 14 lists `lib/payment/{wallet,payout,mor-engine,fee-calculator}.ts`, `services/{wallet,mor,payout,subscription,entitlement,coupon,plan,bnpl}/`, `config/payment-methods.ts`, the dashboard and admin pages, `app/api/{wallet,subscriptions,payouts,plans,coupons}/`, `emails/billing/` and `migrations/S14_*`. Not listed but needed: `workers/s14/**`, `config/s14-*.ts` (numbers and settings), and the cron jobs (covered by the `cron/jobs/s14-*` fragment rule). Components of the pages live in `_components` folders inside the owned page folders. Request R1 asks for the missing rows. Cross-STEP service functions already sanctioned by earlier handoffs (the invoice service of STEP 09) are used as STEP 13 used them; contract methods are requested where they are missing (R4).

D8 (2026-10-19). Ledger design (14.1). Migration `S14_001_wallet_ledger`. A posting (`wallet_postings`, with the idempotency key and a hash of the whole request) has two to twelve legs (`ledger_entries`) that add up to zero; a deferred constraint trigger checks this when the transaction ends, so even a direct insert cannot leave an unbalanced posting. Legs on a company account (`available`, `held`) carry a wallet; legs on the platform accounts (`processor_receivable`, `platform_fee_revenue`, `processor_fee_expense`, `payout_in_transit`, `clearing`) do not. Positive on a company account means the platform owes the company; the platform legs carry the opposite sign. The two balances sit on the wallet row for speed, change only inside `kd_wallet_post` (a trigger refuses any other write), and `kd_wallet_verify()` proves they equal the ledger sums; the assertion and the parallel script both run it. Safety against overdraw is by row locks taken in id order inside the function (not by the serializable isolation level, which PostgREST cannot set per call): two debits of 40 against 100 at the same time give one success and one refusal, never 120 out of 100. The same key is serialized by an advisory lock, so twelve identical requests make one posting and eleven replays (the parallel script found the race that a plain existence check would have had). A debit may take the available balance below zero only when the leg says `floor: false` (a chargeback); a later credit pays the negative balance back automatically. Holds (`wallet_holds`) are posted together with the move from available to held and are released by `kd_wallet_release_hold`, which is safe to call twice.

D9 (2026-10-19). Fees (14.1). `lib/payment/fee-calculator.ts` has four models (flat, percent, percent plus fixed, tiered where the tier of the payment prices the whole payment) with a minimum and a maximum, a schedule with a default model, models per processor and a tax rate on the platform fee, and a breakdown (gross, platform fee, tax, processor fee, what is taken, net). The processor's own fee is a cost of the platform: it is shown but not taken from the company. A fee never exceeds the payment. The platform default is stored in `fee_schedules` (company null) and a company can have its own schedule; both are set only by the super admin (checked in `services/wallet/fees.ts`, audited by the table trigger). Until the platform team sets one, the built-in default is two percent with no fixed part and no tax; it is a starting value, not a price list. Fee history report, the monthly fee invoice and the screens come with 14.2, 14.3.b and 14.6.

D10 (2026-10-19). Wallet alerts and export (14.1). After a posting the owner is told once when the available balance goes below zero and once when it falls below the threshold they set (stored on the wallet). The ledger CSV neutralises cells a spreadsheet would run as formulas. The monthly statement PDF is drawn with the owner page in 14.3.b.

D11 (2026-10-19). Settlement design (14.2). Migration `S14_002_settlement`. A captured payment is settled by `services/mor/settle.ts` into two postings with keys made from the payment (`settle:<payment>` and `settle:<payment>:reserve`): the company receives the payment minus the platform fee and its tax (`held`, or `available` when the hold is zero days), the platform keeps the fees (`platform_fee_revenue`) and the processor owes the whole payment (`processor_receivable`). A reserve (0 to 50 percent of what the company receives, default 0) is a second hold with a later release time (the reserve days). The settlement item (`settlement_items`, one per payment) keeps gross, fee, tax, net, reserve, refunded, charged back, the hold and the reason text the owner sees. Hold length: the standard hold (default 7 days); a company younger than the new-merchant age (default 30 days) gets the longer new-merchant hold (default 14 days); a high automatic risk score adds the risk extension (default 7 days). A refund takes back what the company received for that share of the payment (the platform returns its fee in the same share, once in total) and may take the available balance below zero (later payments pay it back first); a lost dispute takes the disputed amount (not the fee) the same way. Replays are safe because every posting key is made from the payment, refund or dispute id.

D12 (2026-10-19). Mode and risk (14.2). `services/mor/mode.ts` is the one place that decides `mor`, `direct` or `suspended` (rules in `lib/payment/mor-engine.ts`): suspended flag wins; the platform switch (default off) and the company switch (default on) must be on; a verified KYC gives `mor`; an ended verification gives `suspended` (point 1522); missing, pending or rejected KYC gives `direct` with a clear message. A payment captured in `direct` mode is recorded with its fee (for the fee invoice of 14.6); in `suspended` mode it is recorded as `pending` and settled by the hourly job when the mode allows. The contract `kyc` cannot tell an ended verification from one never sent, so `mode.ts` reads `kyc_submissions.expires_at` (read only). Risk is rule based (large payment, payments in 24 hours, daily amount, disputes in 90 days, refund share, new merchant); a score of 70 or more is high: the hold is extended, and when `auto_suspend` is on (default) settlement of new payments is paused and the owner is told. Money already held is never touched by a suspension. Only the super admin changes the settings, suspends or reinstates, releases a hold early or extends one; each action is audited. The MoR agreement e-sign and legal pages (FEATURE_LIST 1513 to 1515) are not in this sub-step; they need the contract and legal page work of the owner pages and are listed in the closure handoff.

D13 (2026-10-19). Reading other STEPs' tables (14.2). `payments` (STEP 10), `disputes` (STEP 13), `companies` and `kyc_submissions` are read only, as STEP 13 read `payments`. No row of those tables is written. The gate for the payment flow is `settlementGate(companyId)` in `services/mor/mode.ts` (request R2).

D14 (2026-10-19). Payouts (14.3.a). Migration `S14_003_payouts`. A payout request does these checks in order: permission, a fresh sign-in confirmation (10 minutes), verified KYC, minimum and fee, active method, active wallet, the period limit (the request row is written first and counted, so two requests at once cannot both pass it) and the funds. The funds leave the available balance at the request (`available` to `payout_in_transit`, the fee to `platform_fee_revenue`), so two requests cannot spend the same money (the ledger locks the wallet). Approval: a payout at or above the large threshold (default 5,000 USD) always waits for the CEO (`payout.large`, level L2, a second person); a staff request below it waits for the owner (`payout.request`, L1); an owner request below it needs none. A rejected, cancelled or failed payout credits the money back once (key `payout:<id>:back`); a paid payout moves the net from `payout_in_transit` to `clearing`; a payout whose money came back after being sent is `reversed` by the super admin. Failed attempts are tried again after 1, 6 and 24 hours (four attempts in all), then the payout fails and the money returns; the platform team can end the retries at once. The FX rate is taken from the `fx` contract at the request and kept on the payout with the local amount (conversion margin from the payout settings). Starting values live in `config/s14-payouts.ts` (minimum 10 USD, limit 10,000 USD per day, fee 0, large threshold 5,000 USD) until the platform team saves its own. The only partner is the manual one (the platform team pays by bank transfer and confirms in the queue with the bank reference); a live partner needs its official documentation and the adapter contract of R3, and `partnerFor` already falls back to the manual partner when a partner is not healthy. A scheduled payout (daily at 06:00 UTC, weekly, or the 1st to 28th of the month) takes the available balance above the amount to keep, stays below the large threshold, and has a key made from the schedule and its run time. Account details are encrypted with purpose `pii` and the company as scope; pages see only "ending 1234". Partner reconciliation, quota monitor and settlement report of a partner (FEATURE_LIST 1372 to 1374) have no live partner to reconcile with and are not built; the queue screens and the payout emails come with 14.3.b.

D15 (2026-10-19). Payment methods (14.4). `config/payment-methods.ts` is the catalogue (internal codes, generic labels, icon names, countries of the Asian markets, lowest plan, standard words for the payer); no brand name appears (the guard and `step14-methods.ts` both check). Table `payment_method_settings` holds two kinds of rows: the platform team's (company empty; per country or every country; on or off, order, fee model, lowest plan, words) and the owner's (on or off, order, words). A method is on for a company only when all three agree: the platform switched it on for the company's country, the plan reaches its lowest plan, and the owner switched it on (`resolveMethods`). The server refuses an owner who tries to switch on a method the platform has not switched on, one the plan does not reach, or one beyond the plan's limit (`METHODS_PER_PLAN`: free 2, pro 4, business 6, enterprise no limit; STEP 14.5 can replace the limit with an entitlement). Owners never enter processor keys. Changing the platform row to off makes the method inactive for every company at once without deleting their choices. The checkout list is `checkoutMethods(companyId)` (ordered, with words and icon); wiring it into the payer's checkout and applying the method fee at settlement are requests to STEP 10 (R9). The QR payment on invoices (FEATURE_LIST 3522) is the method `local_qr_code`; drawing the QR code on the invoice PDF is not done here.

D16 (2026-10-19). Plans and entitlements (14.5). Migration `S14_005_plans`. The database `plans` table (slug, name, prices in USD monthly, yearly and per seat, trial days, visibility, order, badge, archive state) is filled from `config/plans.ts` on first use with prices 0 and the paid plans hidden: the platform team sets the prices and shows the plans (no price is invented). `plan_entitlements` holds a switch (true or false), a limit (number) or no limit (null) per plan and key, with an optional overage price per unit; `plan_history` is the append-only change log. The slug never changes (stored plan keys and routing rules point to it). The free plan cannot be archived; an archived plan is hidden from sign-up and the pricing list but its companies keep it. The `entitlement` contract is now database-backed (`services/entitlement/register.ts`, registered with `override: true`): the plan's saved values lie on top of the built-in values, a key not saved keeps the built-in value, a plan not in the database answers from the built-in catalogue (the free plan for an unknown slug), and a change is answered at once in the same process and within 15 seconds elsewhere. Usage: the four metered keys are counted from their source tables (invoices this month, clients, active seats, storage), so the numbers are always true; `usage_counters` keeps the monthly record for billing the part above the limit (written daily by `cron/jobs/s14-usage.ts`; a billed month is never rewritten) and `kd_usage_add` adds in one step for callers that want a strict atomic quota. A bar is ok under 80 percent, a warning from 80 and blocked at 100. A downgrade never deletes or locks data: `impactOf` lists what is over the new limit and only new work is refused (`assertWithinLimit`, called by the feature that creates things; wiring it into invoice, client and team creation is request R10). `changeCompanyPlan` writes `companies.plan_key` through the service role (the company table belongs to STEP 02; request R11 asks for a tenant-contract method). Feature flags per tenant and user and percentage rollouts (FEATURE_LIST 2681 to 2683) and the "Powered by" badge rules (2684 to 2688) are outside the ranges of this sub-step; the white-label and custom-domain switches already exist as entitlement keys.

D17 (2026-10-19). Platform subscriptions and how a platform invoice is paid (14.6, the open design point). Migration `S14_006_subscriptions`. The payments contract takes an invoice of a company and a payer who is a client of that company, and the invoice, client and approval services read the tenant from the running request, so a cron job or worker cannot create an invoice or a client inside another company from here. Decision: platform invoices are their own documents (`platform_invoices`, numbered PI-YYYY-NNNNNN by `kd_platform_invoice_number`, tax line by the buyer's country, an invoice page that turns into the receipt once paid). Paying today: bank transfer, with instructions from the billing settings; the platform team records the payment against the invoice (`recordBankPayment`), which is idempotent and does what the payment means (starts the first period, applies a paid upgrade, clears a payment problem, rolls a due period forward, sends the receipt). Online card payment: the hooks are built (`operator_invoice_id`, `attachOperatorInvoice`, the worker `subscription.invoice-paid` on `invoice.paid`) and the missing part is request R12 (create and send the invoice of an operator company from a background job). Dunning: the adapter contract has no stored-method charge (R3), so an attempt is a notice and the invoice page stays open; `chargeStoredMethod` is where the processor call goes. Flow: first invoice (a trial starts the plan at once and the first invoice is due at its end; without a trial the plan starts when the invoice is paid); renewal invoice 7 days before the period ends, reminders 7, 3 and 1 days before (each once); a paid period rolls forward (a downgrade or interval change waiting for the end of the cycle is applied then); unpaid after the period end: attempts on the due day, +3 days and +7 days, then 7 days of grace (settable), then the free plan; an unpaid trial ends on the free plan; an upgrade is prorated by the day and applied when its invoice is paid (an unpaid upgrade invoice is cancelled and the plan stays); a downgrade or a change of interval takes effect at the end of the cycle; cancel ends at the end of the cycle with a reason and can be taken back; nothing is ever deleted. Seats above the plan's included seats are billed at the plan's per-seat price. Tax: STEP 09 tax rates belong to one company each, so the platform keeps its own percent by country in the billing settings (default 0) instead. A PDF of the invoice is not drawn (the invoice page prints).

D18 (2026-10-23). Coupons (14.7). Migration `S14_007_coupons`. A coupon has a unique upper-case code (made by the platform team or generated as `KD-XXXXXXXX` without confusing letters), a kind (percent, fixed amount, free trial days), the number of invoices the discount applies to (default 1), a plan restriction, a start and an end time, a total limit and a one-use-per-company switch (on by default). The code, kind and value never change after creation (companies may hold the coupon); the name, plans, dates, limit and the on/off switch can change; a used coupon is switched off, never deleted. Using a coupon is one database function (`kd_coupon_redeem`) that locks the coupon row, checks every rule in a fixed order and adds the redemption, so many companies at once cannot pass the total limit (40 parallel companies on a 5-use coupon give exactly 5) and a one-time coupon works once per company (10 parallel uses give 1). A discount shows on the invoice as a negative line, the tax is on the price after the discount, and the invoice remembers its redemption (`coupon_redemption_id`, `discount_amount`); the number of invoices already made with a redemption is counted, so a retry never uses an invoice twice and renewal invoices carry the discount until the coupon's invoices are used up. A free trial coupon adds trial days and grants a trial even when the company had one before. A coupon is used only for a plan that costs something. Guard: wrong codes are recorded (hash of the code only) and after 10 in an hour a company is asked to wait. Reports: uses per coupon in a period, the discount given and the paid revenue of the invoices that used it. Referral credit codes (credit to the company that shared a code) are not built: they need an account credit that reduces later invoices, which is a larger change to invoice payment than this sub-step; recorded for the closure handoff. A redemption is not given back when its first invoice is cancelled unpaid (a known simplification).

D19 (2026-10-23). Client subscriptions (14.8.a and 14.8.b). Migration `S14_008_client_subscriptions`. A company defines recurring plans (price in USD, monthly or yearly, features shown to the client, trial days, a price per extra seat above the included seats). A client joins a plan: the price, seat price and included seats are copied to the subscription, so a later change of the plan does not change what current subscribers pay. Only the owner manages subscriptions (staff can look). Without a trial the first invoice is made at once; with a trial it is made when the trial ends. Each cycle: the cycle is claimed first (`client_billing_runs`, unique per subscription, kind and period start; 30 parallel jobs give one claim), the invoice is made through `createInvoice` of STEP 09 using the company's own blank invoice defaults (`loadFormData`), the client's billing details and one line, and is sent with `sendInvoice` as the owner who created the subscription (the same way the recurring schedules of STEP 09 work from a background job, with no request around). The subscription then moves to its next period. If the invoice is made but not sent, the cycle is not repeated and the owner can send it by hand. A plan or seat change that costs more starts now and the difference is invoiced by the day (the same proration as the platform plans); a cheaper change starts with the next cycle; during a trial the change is simply made. Pause keeps the subscription but moves the next invoice by the time paused and can resume on a date; cancel ends at the end of the paid cycle (with a reason) or at once; a cancellation can be withdrawn. An unpaid cycle invoice gives three notices to the owner on the due day, +3 and +7 days (the invoice service reminds the client by its own reminder job), then 7 days of grace, then the subscription ends as not paid; a payment brings it back to active. Renewal reminders (7, 3, 1 days) go to the owner. Reports: MRR (yearly counted per month), yearly figure, subscribers, revenue per subscriber, churn in a period and lifetime value. Not built: charging a stored card by itself (the adapter contract cannot, R3) and the client's own pause or cancel on the portal (the portal belongs to STEP 13, request R13); both are listed in the closure handoff.

D20 (2026-10-23). Installments, "buy now, pay later" (14.9). Migration `S14_009_bnpl`. Built as a framework on top of the installment plans of STEP 09: the parts of a plan are the installment rows of the invoice, so each payment the client makes covers the oldest unpaid part and no second payment path exists. The service is off until the platform team switches it on (platform row of `bnpl_settings`) and the company switches it on for itself; a company can narrow the allowed numbers of parts (2, 3, 4, 6, 9, 12, 18 or 24; at most 6 offered) and the invoice amounts, never widen them. Starting values: parts 3, 6 and 12, invoices from 50 to 5,000 USD, one paid invoice needed first, a part is late after 3 days, a plan is in default after 2 late parts, the smallest part 10 USD. An invoice is offered installments when it was sent and is unpaid with nothing paid yet and no plan, the client is not high risk and has no overdue invoice, and the client has enough paid invoices. The owner sees the reasons a client is not offered; the client never sees a score. The client always pays the invoice amount and nothing more; the fee is paid by the seller, as a percent per number of parts set by the platform team (starting at 0 until a price is set). A plan is one row per invoice (unique), keeps the consent text and who accepted (the owner today), and a history of events. A daily check completes a plan when all parts are paid, marks it defaulted after the late-part limit (the owner is told, nothing is taken from the client and the invoice stays open for the normal reminders), and a refund on the invoice is written to the plan history. The only partner is `in_house` (the seller carries the risk); a partner that pays the seller up front and takes the risk needs its official documentation first (rule 8) and the stored-method and payout parts of R3. Fee collection from the seller's wallet at settlement is not wired: the fee is recorded on the plan and reported, and charging it belongs to the settlement step once a risk-carrying partner exists.
