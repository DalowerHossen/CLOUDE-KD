# STEP 09 decisions

- D1 (plan) The SCOPE lists 11 sub-steps; they are split into 17 so that each stays under 15 files and one migration group. Order follows dependencies: calculation, tax and FX, invoice core, numbering, screens, send and contract, PDF, then the document families, billing lifecycle, projects, dashboard, reports, batch, closure.
- D2 (plan) Public client pages (viewing an invoice by token, approving an estimate with a signature) are STEP 13 (`app/(client-portal)`, services `portal` and `esign`). STEP 09 issues tokens through the `tokens` contract and provides the service functions the portal calls; the missing contract operations (mark viewed, record an estimate decision) go to DEPENDENCY_REQUESTS.
- D3 (plan) Data that belongs to STEP 10 (payments by method and gateway) and STEP 15 (expenses, cash flow, AP aging, accounting) reaches the reports only through a provider registry in `lib/reports/providers.ts`; until a provider registers, those report sections show a clear "no data from that module yet" state. STEP 09 never reads another STEP's tables.
- D4 (09.1) Calculation engine (`lib/invoice/calc.ts`, pure, decimal arithmetic only). Per line: price times quantity, then the line discount (percent or a fixed amount that can never turn the line around), then the share of the document discount (a fixed document discount is shared over the lines with a positive amount in proportion to their amounts; the last line takes the remainder so the shares add up exactly), then tax. Discount order is a setting: "before tax" (discounts lower the taxable amount) or "after tax" (tax is worked out on the amount before discounts; the discount only lowers what is payable). Prices may include tax ("inclusive"); the tax is then taken out with an exact factor (`taxMultiplier`, twelve decimal places, integer arithmetic) and the last tax absorbs the rounding so net plus tax is exactly the entered price. Several taxes per line, each marked compound or not (a compound tax is worked out on the amount plus the taxes before it). A tax-exempt line carries no tax.
- D5 (09.1) Rounding is a document setting: round every line and tax ("per line", default) or only the document totals ("on total"; the engine then reports `roundingDifference`, the gap between the rounded lines and the rounded total, for the reconciliation line); rule "half up" or banker's "half even"; precision 2, 3 or 4 places. Six places are not offered: money is stored as numeric(18,4). Optional cash rounding of the final total to 0.05, 0.10, 0.50 or 1 is shown as its own adjustment line. Withholding tax is a percentage of the amount without tax (including shipping); it lowers `amountDue` but not the total.
- D6 (09.1) Amount in words is English ("One Hundred Twenty-Three USD and Forty-Five Cents Only"), up to 15 digits, "Minus" for negatives. The verification script `scripts/verify/step09-calc.ts` holds the hand-calculated cases (0.1 + 0.2, inclusive tax, discount order, compound tax, rounding policies, shipping, withholding, cash rounding, negative and zero invoices) and a check on 2000 generated invoices that the parts always add up to the total.
- D7 (09.2) Tax data (migration S09_001): `tax_rates` (code unique per company, percentage `numeric(7,4)`, kinds sales, VAT, GST, withholding and other, optional compound flag, country and region, active flag), `tax_settings` (one row per company: price mode, discount order, rounding policy, rule and places, cash rounding, financial year start, seller tax number and country, EU reverse-charge switch, marketplace-facilitator declaration, US sales-tax states, default tax codes on new lines, default withholding) and `client_tax_exemptions` (exempt flag with reason, certificate file through the storage contract, certificate expiry, client tax number). The calculation defaults of D4 and D5 are read from `tax_settings`. A change to a rate never changes an existing invoice: invoices copy the rates they use. Country templates (India GST, Malaysia SST, Vietnam VAT, Singapore, Thailand, Philippines, Indonesia, Japan, South Korea, Bangladesh, Pakistan, China, United Kingdom, Australia, United Arab Emirates) are one-click suggestions to be checked with a tax adviser; India e-invoicing (IRN), fapiao and TDS/TCS filing are not built (SCOPE: display only).
- D8 (09.2) EU reverse charge is a pure rule (`reverseChargeApplies`): switched on in the settings, seller and client in different EU countries, client has a tax number. The VAT-number check (VIES) is a hook (`registerTaxIdChecker`); without a registered service the number is stored as entered and shown as "not checked". The tax pages live under `/invoices/tax` (the settings area of STEP 07 is not ours).
- D9 (09.2) Exchange rates (`fx_rates`, units of currency per US dollar, `numeric(20,8)`): platform rates have no company, a company can type in its own rates; the newest rate on or before the asked day wins, and on the same day the company's rate wins (`kd_fx_rate`). Stored money stays USD; conversion is for display only (`convertUsd`, two places, none for JPY, VND and IDR). The daily job `fx-rates` asks the registered rate providers (`lib/fx/provider.ts`). No provider is registered: the rate source's own documentation could not be reached from this environment (the host is blocked), and a provider must be written from that documentation, so until then companies type their own rates (DEPENDENCY_REQUESTS). The `fx` contract is registered in `services/fx/register.ts`. FX gain and loss belongs to accounting (STEP 15).
- D10 (09.3) Invoice tables (migration S09_002): `invoices` (header with frozen calculation options, frozen totals, tax breakdown, bill-to and ship-to blocks, seller block frozen at send, display currency with the frozen rate, duplicate hash, status fields), `invoice_items` (lines with their worked-out amounts and the tax rates copied onto the line), `invoice_revisions` and `invoice_events` (append only), `invoice_payment_links` (one row per payment applied, unique per payment and invoice, so applying twice is harmless). One table holds invoices, proforma invoices, deposit invoices and debit notes (`kind`); credit notes and estimates have their own tables later. The invoice number is empty on a draft and is given when the invoice is sent (09.4), so numbers have no gaps.
- D11 (09.3) A sent invoice is a legal record. The database trigger `kd_invoice_guard` lets a draft change freely; once the invoice is waiting for approval, sent or later, only status fields, payment fields, internal notes, tags, category and the approval and sending fields can change - client, dates, amounts, rates, notes seen by the client are refused (error 42501), the number and the seller block cannot change once set, and a sent invoice cannot be deleted (only a void one can go to the trash). Lines are locked by `kd_invoice_item_guard` as soon as the invoice leaves draft. Corrections to a sent invoice are made with a credit note or by voiding and writing a new invoice.
- D12 (09.3) The server never trusts totals: the input has no total fields; `prepareInvoice` turns tax codes into the company's current rates (copied onto the lines), applies a client's tax exemption (unless the certificate has expired) and the EU reverse charge (all tax removed, flag kept), freezes the exchange rate for the display currency on the issue date (error when no rate exists) and calculates with the engine of D4. The calculation options (price mode, discount order, rounding) are copied from the tax settings by the screen and stored on the invoice, so a later change of settings never changes an old invoice. Bill-to details are typed or copied by the screen from the client record (the `clients` contract only offers name and billing postal code and country); the contract is used to check that the client is the company's own.
- D13 (09.3) Revisions: every manual save (and create, and a restore) writes a revision holding the full input; the automatic save every 30 seconds updates the draft without a revision. A revision can be restored into the draft (as a new save). Status override is owner-only, needs a reason, follows the status machine (`lib/invoice/status.ts`; a sent invoice never returns to draft, a paid invoice can only become disputed, a void invoice is final) and is audited. Money columns use the helper `toInt` of `lib/reports/numbers.ts` for whole numbers because the money guard forbids `Number()` in files with money words in their path.
- D14 (09.4) Numbering (migration S09_003): `invoice_sequences` holds one counter per company, series (INV, PI, DEP, DN) and year (0 when the series never resets). `kd_invoice_assign_number` locks the invoice, takes the next value under an advisory lock per company and series (`kd_next_doc_number`) and writes the number on the invoice in the same transaction, so a failed save gives the number back and parallel requests never share one (proved by `scripts/verify/step09-numbering.sh`: 100 parallel numbers are 1 to 100 with no gap, a rolled-back number is reused). A draft has no number; it is given at send or approval (09.6). Format and start come from the company's invoice settings of STEP 07 (prefix, yearly or continuous numbering, digits, first number); the first number of the company starts the series, a new year of a yearly series starts at 1, and the year is the year of sending, not of the issue date. Proforma, deposit and debit note series use the fixed prefixes PI-, DEP- and DN-. Voided documents keep their number; numbers are never reused. The owner can type a number on a draft (unique per company and kind, uses no counter value, cannot be typed once the invoice is sent).
- D15 (09.5) Invoice list: search runs in the database function `kd_invoice_search` (migration S09_004): number, purchase order number, client name with typo tolerance (word similarity), exact amount; filters for state (one or several), type, client, issue date range and amount range; sort by issue date, due date, total, state, client, number or creation; cursor pages of 25, 50 or 100; the trash view lists deleted drafts and void invoices. Rows can be shown as a table or as cards. A "Late" mark shows on open invoices past their due date. Bulk selection and bulk actions come with 09.16.
- D16 (09.5) Invoice form: everything the client side shows is only a preview. The form works the totals out with the same engine (`previewTotals`), but the server works them out again on every save. The form copies the client's bill-to and ship-to blocks and usual payment terms when a client is chosen (through the client API of STEP 08, read-only), supports walk-in customers, dynamic lines (items from the catalogue picker with the client's price, free lines, move up and down, drag to reorder, per-line discount, tax chips and a "no tax" switch), windowed rendering above 60 lines so an invoice with thousands of lines stays fast, a live totals panel with the amount in words, the tax breakdown and the display-currency amount at the frozen rate, and an automatic save every 30 seconds for an existing draft (no revision is written by an automatic save). A new invoice is created with an Idempotency-Key so a double click makes one draft. Conflicts show the plain "someone else changed this" message. The invoice page shows the same form read-only for sent invoices, with the history (timeline and earlier versions with a comparison and a restore button for drafts) and the actions (copy, trash, restore, owner-only manual number and manual status).
- D17 (09.6) Sending (migration S09_005): `kd_invoice_send` checks the invoice (at least one line, a client or customer name, billing postal code and country, no negative total), gives it its number if it has none, freezes the seller block and sets the status in one transaction. A zero invoice becomes paid at once. The owner sends immediately; a staff member's "send" submits the invoice for approval (rule `invoice.send`, level L1) and the job `invoice-approvals` (every 5 minutes) sends it after the owner approves, or returns it to draft with the reason; staff can take an invoice back from approval. The seller block holds the company profile, branding, tax number, terms, footer and the bank details. Bank details ("Remit to") are kept encrypted (`pii`, scope company) in the tax settings row and are printed on invoices, so they are also part of the frozen seller block of each sent invoice (the invoice is the legal document). Sending e-mails the client a link (template `invoice.sent`, the link is `/i/<token>`, the portal of STEP 13) when the bill-to block has an e-mail address; without one the invoice is still sent and the screen says so.
- D18 (09.6) Duplicate warning: invoices of the same type, client (or walk-in name), total and issue date share a hash; sending an invoice that has look-alikes answers 409 with the list until the person confirms. Void: owner only, with a reason; a paid invoice cannot be voided (write a credit note); void publishes `invoice.voided` with the lines so stock is given back; a partly paid invoice can be voided and the screen reminds the owner to write a credit note.
- D19 (09.6) Payments (`services/invoice/payments.ts`): `getForPayment` returns the amount due (zero unless the invoice is open), the billing postal code and country frozen in the bill-to block, status and version; `applyPayment` is atomic and idempotent per payment (`kd_invoice_apply_payment`; the same payment twice changes nothing, more than the balance is refused so the payment STEP keeps any surplus as a client credit); `markDisputed`; `recordInvoiceViewed` for the client page. Events published: `invoice.created`, `invoice.sent`, `invoice.viewed`, `invoice.paid`, `invoice.partially_paid`, `invoice.voided`, `invoice.overdue` (the hourly job `invoice-overdue` marks late invoices). `invoice.sent` and `invoice.voided` carry `invoiceId` and `lines` for the inventory worker of STEP 08. The `invoices` contract is registered together with `snapshot` in 09.7. The contract has no operation for "viewed" or "estimate decision"; DEPENDENCY_REQUESTS asks for them (STEP 13 can already call `recordInvoiceViewed`).

## D20 - PDF engine (09.7)
- One layout component (`lib/pdf/invoice-pdf.tsx`) with four looks; data is built first (`lib/pdf/invoice/data.ts`) and stored with the snapshot, so the layout can change without changing history.
- Built-in Helvetica is the default font (Western European only; other characters become "?"). A Unicode font is used when `PDF_FONT_DIR` holds `regular.ttf` and `bold.ttf` (subsetted by the PDF library). Fonts are not committed (licence/size); listed in DEPENDENCY_REQUESTS.
- Logo is downsampled with sharp (max 320x192, PNG) before drawing; PDFs are limited to 10 MB.
- A sent invoice returns the stored snapshot (exact document sent); drafts are drawn fresh with a DRAFT watermark. Snapshot at send is best effort; job `invoice-snapshots` (10 min) retries. One `sent` snapshot per invoice, append only.
- `pdf` contract: kinds are registered with `registerPdfRenderer`; STEP 09 registers `invoice`. Other kinds belong to other STEPs.
- `@react-pdf/renderer` loads only as ESM: the verify script bundles the layout with esbuild first (`scripts/verify/step09-pdf.mts`). Next.js may need `serverExternalPackages: ['@react-pdf/renderer','sharp']` (Integrator, next.config).

## D21 - Estimates and proforma (09.8)
- Estimates are their own table (`estimates`, `estimate_events`), not invoices: their statuses differ (accepted, declined, expired, converted) and they must never touch invoice numbers or payments. The form input of an invoice is stored as-is, so an accepted estimate converts without retyping. The estimate form is the invoice form; its "due date" is "valid until".
- Numbering: series `EST` in `invoice_sequences`, prefix `EST-`, same padding and yearly rule as invoices, gap-free (assigned at send, one transaction).
- A sent estimate is fixed by trigger. The owner or staff can take it back to a draft until the client decides. A decision (accept or decline) is final: signature record = typed name, time, network address, browser, SHA-256 of (estimate content + decision + name + time + address). The checksum is stored on the row.
- Client page `/e/<token>` belongs to STEP 13. STEP 09 provides `getEstimateByToken` (first look marks it viewed) and `decideEstimate(token, decision, {ip, agent})` in `services/estimate/public.ts`. The caller (STEP 13) takes the address and browser from the request.
- Events: `estimate.sent`, `estimate.approved`, `estimate.rejected` (names fixed by the platform event list).
- Converting an accepted estimate creates a draft invoice through `createInvoice` (same checks as a hand-written one). If a tax rate changed since, the invoice total differs and the screen says so.
- Proforma invoices stay in `invoices` (kind `proforma`, series `PI`). "Make an invoice" copies a sent proforma to a new draft that points back through `parent_invoice_id`; one conversion per proforma.
- Estimate PDF uses the invoice layout with the title "ESTIMATE", "Valid until" and no payment details; it is not stored as a snapshot.

## D22 - Credit notes, debit notes, deposits (09.9)
- Credit notes are their own table (`credit_notes`) with a gap-free series CN (prefix `CN-`). They are written from a sent invoice (kind invoice or debit note): all of it (frozen figures copied) or chosen quantities of chosen lines (worked out again with the tax rates and discounts the invoice carried, not today's rates). Per-line limit: quantity on the invoice minus quantity in other draft or issued credit notes. Total limit (database): credit notes of an invoice never exceed its total.
- Issuing makes the note fixed (trigger). By default it is applied at once to the invoice it came from (up to the invoice balance). What is left stays as client credit and can be applied to other open invoices of the same client (`credit_applications`, append only).
- An application counts as a payment of the invoice (`kd_invoice_apply_payment` with the application id as payment id) inside the same transaction, so status and balance change at once. Events `invoice.paid` / `invoice.partially_paid` carry `source: credit_note|deposit`. Refunding credit to cash is a payment-STEP matter (STEP 10); STEP 09 only keeps the credit balance.
- A credit note that was applied cannot be voided (reverse by writing a debit note). Only the owner issues, applies and voids (permissions credit.issue, credit.apply, credit.void).
- Deposits: an invoice of kind `deposit` (series DEP). Created from an invoice (a percentage of every line, or one fixed amount line) or from a sent estimate. A paid deposit is applied to the final invoice of the same client through `credit_applications` (source type deposit); the deposit is not reduced, only counted as used.
- Debit notes: invoice kind `debit_note` (series DN), written as a draft from a sent invoice (`parent_invoice_id`), then edited and sent like any invoice.
- Stock: `return_to_stock` is stored on the credit note. STEP 08 listens only to `invoice.sent` / `invoice.voided`; a stock return for credit notes needs an agreed event, listed in DEPENDENCY_REQUESTS. STEP 09 does not call STEP 08 code.

## D23 - Recurring invoices and installments (09.10)
- A schedule copies an invoice template (made from a real invoice with "Repeat this invoice"). The repeat rule is a unit (day, week, month, year) and an interval; every date is worked out from the start date and the occurrence number, so the 31st does not drift (Jan 31, Feb 28, Mar 31). Optional "last day of the month" rule. End date and maximum count are both supported. The rule of a schedule that already ran cannot change (end it and start a new one); name, end rule, message and auto-send can.
- Exactly-once: each occurrence is claimed in the database (unique schedule + occurrence number, row lock with skip-locked); an invoice made by a run carries the run id (unique), so a retry after a crash finds it. A failed or stale (15 min) claim can be claimed again; three failures in a row pause the schedule and notify the owner.
- Due dates use each company's own calendar day (time zone from the company profile). At most three missed occurrences are caught up per job run (each dated with its own scheduled day). Companies that are not active are skipped.
- Auto-send is allowed only on a schedule created by the owner; staff schedules always produce drafts (the invoice send approval rule stays in force). If sending fails the invoice stays a draft and the run records the reason.
- Placeholders {month}, {year}, {date}, {number} in notes and line descriptions are filled per invoice.
- Installments: parts add up exactly to the amount due (last part takes the rounding rest), optional down payment, even split by weeks or months, or hand-typed dates and amounts (2 to 60 parts). Payments are not matched to parts; parts are covered in date order from the money paid, so partial payments and overpaying early parts need no extra bookkeeping. The overdue sweep also marks an invoice overdue when a part is past its date and not covered. Replacing a plan keeps one active plan per invoice.
- Template editing of an existing schedule is not offered (change the template by ending the schedule and starting a new one from an updated invoice).

## D24 - Reminders, late fees, write-offs (09.11)
- Reminders: a ladder of day offsets from the due date (default -3, 0, 7, 14, 30). One e-mail per step per invoice (`reminder_log`, unique, append only). Sent in the company's time zone, outside quiet hours (default 20:00 to 08:00) and, by default, on business days only. At most one step goes out per run (the newest due one); older unsent steps are logged as skipped. A step more than three days late is skipped, so switching reminders on never floods old invoices. Disputed invoices, invoices with an active hold (promise to pay until a date, or a pause) and invoices without a client e-mail get nothing. "Tell me when a reminder is sent" is an in-app notification (the e-mail contract has no copy field).
- Promise to pay: a hold with a date; after the date passes without payment reminders resume by themselves (no extra job). The owner or staff can release a hold.
- Late fees: owner-only rule (percent of the unpaid balance or fixed; once or every 30 days; grace days; caps by percent of invoice total and/or amount). The daily job proposes only the newest due period of an invoice, never earlier ones (no retroactive fees), and never on a debit note (no fee on a fee). A proposed fee waits for the owner, or is applied by itself when the owner chose "apply without asking" (the schedule then works as the owner who saved the rule). Applying creates a debit note (no tax) and sends it. Waiving a fee that was applied voids its debit note.
- Write-off: owner only, reason of at least 10 characters, up to the unpaid balance. It is a settlement recorded in `invoice_writeoffs` and `credit_applications` (source type `write_off`) and counted as a payment of the invoice in one transaction, so the invoice closes; reports can separate bad debt from cash. Event payload carries `source: write_off`. Staff cannot write off (no approval request flow needed; the permission is owner-only and audited).

## D25 - Projects and time (09.12)
- A project is billed to a client (hourly, fixed fee, retainer hours, or not billed). Whoever creates a project is its manager; the owner manages all. Managers (and the owner) change the project, its team, rates and milestones and approve time of that project. Team names come from the company member list (`services/team/members.listMembers`, read only; a `team` contract would be cleaner and is listed in DEPENDENCY_REQUESTS).
- A person can log time only on projects they are a member of (the owner on any), and not on completed or cancelled projects. The rate is frozen on the entry when it is made: the person's rate on the project, else the project rate, else zero. Non-billable projects log non-billable time.
- Time entries: draft -> submitted -> approved -> invoiced; rejected goes back to the person with a reason. From "submitted" on, hours, date, rate and text are fixed by a trigger. Nobody approves their own time except the owner. If the company switches approval off, submitting approves at once. The owner can lock a period: no entry on or before the lock date can be added, changed or deleted.
- One running timer per person (unique index). Stopping rounds the time up to the company step (0, 5, 6, 10, 15, 30 or 60 minutes), at least 0.01 hours, at most 24 hours (a forgotten timer is cut at one day and left as a draft to correct).
- Budget use is shown in hours and in dollars (warning from 80 percent, "used up" from 100). Billing of approved time is 09.13.

## D26 - Billing project work (09.13)
- A manager (or the owner) bills a project from its "Bill" page or the "Time waiting to be billed" list. The result is always a draft invoice for the project client, with the usual default tax codes; sending follows the normal rules (staff invoices wait for the owner).
- Exactly once: each approved billable hour is claimed in the database (`kd_time_claim`, status "invoiced" without invoice) before the invoice is made, then linked. If the invoice cannot be made the claim is released; a claim that never got an invoice is released by the hourly job after an hour. Deleting a draft or voiding an invoice gives its time, milestones and expenses back.
- Hours can show as one line per entry, per day, per person, or one line in all. A single rate can be set for the whole invoice (it applies to the invoice lines only; the entries keep the rate they were logged with).
- Fixed fee projects are billed by milestone (a milestone must be done and have an amount); their hours count only against the budget. Retainer projects bill the fee of the period (the project's fixed fee field) as one line plus the hours above the included hours at the hourly rate; hours inside the retainer are marked billed without a line of their own. Hours do not roll over between periods.
- Reimbursable expenses: a small provider hook (`services/project/expense-hook.ts`). The expense STEP registers its provider; until then none are offered and STEP 09 reads no expense tables. DEPENDENCY_REQUESTS lists it.
- The client address on the draft comes from the clients contract (`getBilling`: name, e-mail, postal code, country); the person completes the rest on the draft.

## D27 - Dashboard (09.14)
- `/dashboard` is the home page of a company. Numbers are added up in the database (`kd_dashboard_stats`, `kd_dashboard_series`; money as text) for the chosen period (this/last month, quarter, year, last 30 days, last year) and the period before it, so every card can say "compared with". Days are UTC days; a company time zone is not applied to dashboard sums (documented limit).
- "Collected" is cash only: payments applied to invoices, excluding credit notes, deposits and write-offs (these are shown apart as credited / written off). "Outstanding" is amount due minus paid on open invoices; aging buckets are by days past the due date (not due, 1-30, 31-60, 61-90, over 90).
- Charts use Recharts (client side) with the design tokens `--chart-1..5`, a text alternative (`role="img"` with label) and a hidden data table with the same numbers. Chart bars take plain numbers through `plotValue` (drawing only; every amount shown as text comes from Money).
- Widgets: search, quick actions, key numbers, invoiced vs collected, aging, needs attention, recent activity, my time. Each person can hide and reorder them (saved per person and company in `dashboard_layouts`; moves are buttons, so they work with a keyboard). New widgets appear at the end of a saved layout; widgets the person may not use are left out. Every widget can go full screen with the browser Fullscreen API.
- Global search: `/api/reports/search` finds invoices, estimates, credit notes and projects (each kind only if the person may read it). Clients are found by the page through the existing `/api/clients` API, so STEP 09 does not read client tables.
- The "latest payments" list shows every settlement of an invoice, including credits (the dashboard counts cash separately in the cards).

## D28 - Reports (09.15)
- Catalogue (owner by default; the owner can grant staff `report.read`, `report.export`, `report.schedule`): sales summary, revenue by client, revenue by item, invoices by status, payments received, unpaid invoices by age, credit notes, tax summary, time (by project or person), estimates. Other STEPs add reports (payments by method, expenses, profit and loss, cash flow) with `registerReport`; nothing here reads their tables.
- Every report is a pure function over plain rows (`lib/reports/aggregate.ts`) so each total can be checked by hand (see VERIFY). Rows are fetched per company, at most 20000 per report; a larger period is refused with a clear message. Amounts stay text from the database to the file.
- Definitions: a sale is an invoice or debit note that was sent in the period and is not void; credit notes count on their issue date and void ones are ignored; "collected" is cash (payments applied to invoices, without credits, deposits and write-offs); aging counts days from the due date; tax comes from the tax breakdown frozen on each invoice and credit note.
- Comparison: totals of the period against the period before (previous month, quarter or year; the same number of days for custom dates and "last 30 days"), with the percentage change, or "n/a" when the earlier total is zero.
- Exports: CSV (with a byte order mark, formula-neutralised cells, and a last line with company, person, time and export id), Excel (.xlsx written without a library: text is text, never a formula; amounts and counts are numbers; header row bold and frozen) and PDF (landscape table with repeated header and page numbers). Every export is audited.
- Presets are per person; schedules belong to the company. A schedule uses a named period (for example "last month"), so each run covers a fresh period. The job files the report (encrypted, category "export") and e-mails a download link that works for seven days; the file is never attached. The run date is moved before the work starts, so a day is never run twice; three failures in a row switch the schedule off and tell the owner.
- Not in this version: recurring-invoice forecast and project profitability with cost rates (no cost rates exist), expense and profit reports (STEP 15).
